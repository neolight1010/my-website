I decided not to use AI for building this website, and for any of my personal
projects, for that matter. I will try to explain some of my reasons why.

## This website

I made this website as an experiment, an exercise, and a piece of art.

1. An experiment on visual design and communication.
2. An exercise on simplicity, both technical and on aesthetics.
3. A piece of art, a way to express myself.

I explictly chose to build this site on a minimal blog engine and vanilla
HTML, CSS and JavaScript. I chose to design every layout and to write every
line by hand. Each color and style was carefully thought out.

Now, I don't claim this site is superior because of that. In fact, you might describe it as
"simplistic", or "ugly" even. But that's alright. With this project I don't
aim to ship, or to satisfy any userbase. I just want to learn, to make, to have
fun and to express myself sincerely.

Actually, that's why I fell in love with software in the first place. Because,
for me, it's more than mere text. It's art.

## Software as an art

"Art" is a very subjective concept. Let me narrow it down a bit (just a bit),
on what I mean here. I seek beauty, thoughtfulness, and sincerity. I think
that a work that is beautiful, thoughtful and sincere, is a true masterpiece.

(this is what it means **to me**; I don't claim to be objective.)

Here are some examples of what I think are amazing pieces of art that fit these
criteria:

- **[Spider-Man: Into/Across the Spider-Verse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man:_Into_the_Spider-Verse):** Very rarely you see this amount of effort and thoughtfulness in a multi-million dollar movie.
- **[Kane Pixel's Backrooms](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H4dGpz6cnHo&list=PLVAh-MgDVqvDUEq6qDXqORBioE4Yhol_z):** Amazing storytelling, thorough world-building, wonderful visuals, incredible technical skill.
- **[Liszt's Liebestraum No. 3](https://youtu.be/FZ651tNXp0Y?si=LtGcKV0uFoZEtHAq):** You could pick any piece by Liszt, actually; but this one is the most beautiful to me.

*But that's music and movies. What about software?*

I think software engineering has its place as an art-form, which I demonstrate by the existence of these projects:

- **[The Linux kernel](https://kernel.org/):** Perhaps the biggest collaboration project in human history. Despite the amount of contributions, it remains a thoroughly well crafted piece of software.
- **[SQLite](https://sqlite.org/index.html):** The most used database in the whole world. A marvelous piece of engineering, kept as so by the passionate and caring work of its maintainers, as demonstrated by their one-of-a-kind [Code of Ethics](https://sqlite.org/codeofethics.html).
- **[Wikipedia/Wikimedia](https://doc.wikimedia.org/):** A true testament of the power of software for the good of humanity.
- **[Haskell](https://www.haskell.org/):** One of the most elegantly designed programming languages, with one of the most powerful compilers out there. A true technical wonder.

Software and engineering as an art has also been explored in the following works:

- **[Richard Hamming's The Art of Doing Science and Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Art_of_Doing_Science_and_Engineering):** A must-read for all engineers. Transcend the technical to achieve greatness.
- **[Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_and_Interpretation_of_Computer_Programs):** The book "dedicated to the spirit that lives in the computer". Allan Perlis' introduction expresses neatly the need of art in computing: "I hope the field of computer science never loses its sense of fun".
- **[Daniel Shiffman's The Nature of Code](https://natureofcode.com/):** "Art, as far as it is able, follows nature", and this book perfectly captures how code can follow nature to make beautiful art pieces.
- **[Donald Knuth's The Art of Computer Programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Art_of_Computer_Programming):** The magnum opus of one of the most influential engineers in history.

As I see it, art in software is evident in the following aspects:

1. **Low level code and design:** Do the algorithms avoid unnecessary repetition?
  Do the interfaces build an expressive and powerful domain language that helps
  solve the required problems?
2. **High level design & architecture:** Is the system's architecture well structured,
  so that it's flexible and performant?
3. **The API:** Does the API's design allow clients to interact with the system with
  low complexity and high ease of use?
4. **The user interface:** Is the user interface intuitive and accessible? Does it
  avoid unnecessary distractions and represent hierarchy appropriately?
5. **The effort and care:** Was the system built with actual care by its
  developers? Was it built with the true intention of serving its users?
  Was it made just for pure economical gain, or does it go beyond it?

Vibe-coded software (notice the term "vibe-coded") won't qualify on any of these aspects.

"Yeah, that's very romantic and all, but what ultimately matters is that the software
works, right?". Well...

## But what's the point?

If a piece of software works, and it does what it's supposed to, then what does
it matter how it was built? I have a few opinions about that way of thinking.

### The "pragmatic" viewpoint

Let's tackle the hard technical aspects first.

Thinking that a piece of software is merely the final executable product is a very narrow
view of software engineering. A piece of software is, first and foremost, a product
of the people who built it.

For decades it's been true that the knowledge of a system is coupled to its developers.
Of course, you can reduce this to a minimum by applying good design practices, but this
cannot be 100% eliminated. Even if AI gets so good that it understands 100% of the code,
it won't be able to understand the reasoning behind each decision behind the code. Read
[Peter Naur's Programming as Theory Building](https://ingenieria-de-software-i.github.io/assets/bibliografia/programming-as-theory-building.pdf)
for a more nuanced explanation of this model.

Now, a software product built with care and intention by its developers is one
that will tend to apply better technical practices, ultimately leading to a
better quality product than one built recklessly and with little attention.
Linux would not have gone as far as it did if it were not for Linus Torvald's
true care. Git wouldn't be such a good piece of software if its developers
didn't care enough to painstakingly review every contribution.

For AI coding, code crafted with elegance will utilize less tokens for an LLM,
allowing for better context window management that improves the behavior
of the model. And, LLMs will follow the style already established, so elegant
code has a better chance of generating more elegant code.

Finally, a software engineer that practices coding and design, like you practice an art, will learn its
technical skills more intricately than an engineer who doesn't. This will also help develop professional intuition.
These deep skills and intuition will lead to building better quality software in professional enviroments.

### The artistic viewpoint

Let's not get side-tracked. This post was about art. And the final purpose
of art is not *just* economical gain. Remember that Linux was made [just for fun](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/160171.Just_for_Fun),

I like [Kane Parson's defintion of art](https://youtu.be/POFghn4C6vk?si=PuLjbAu8F9dY7nUp&t=336): "Art is a way of processing life... I don't see any value in outsourcing
any element of that".

I think of software the same way. You can use code to build programs that art artful all throughout, all the way from their source code to their user interface. I think we should all aim to do it, to show the [magnificence of humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnifica_humanitas), especially in this age where most code is slop-generated with little thought and care.

## At work, it's different

I must clarify: I am not saying you cannot build good quality software with AI.
I think we're way past the point where we need to argue whether AI works or not.
The fact is that AI-generated code *works*. But, as I've made clear earlier,
I think a great craftsman, and a great engineer, needs to go *past* merely working.

From my experience, I have come to think of coding agents as *multipliers*. A
good engineer will multiply their productivity using AI, without comprimising
the quality of the output. A bad engineer, however, will have their output
volume multiplied positively but its quality multiplied negatively.

If you learn the art first, **then** you will be able to take advantage of these
"advanced" tools appropriately.

And that's the point I want to make. At work, you definitely should use AI
tools; but you **must not neglect** the essence of our craft. AI tools have multiplied
the speed we can produce code, and along it the necessity of solid technical
skillsd and professional intuition, which are built through artistry and practice.

## Summary

With this post I've wanted to highlight my personal reasons of rejecting AI in
non-professional settings. I made the point that software and coding are themselves
art forms, and if you want to master the art, you should avoid code-generation
tools.

I encourage everyone to make software as art, not only because it will enhance
your skills technically, but as a testament of human creativity and intelligence
in the era of slop.


## Annex: the spectrum of AI code generation

You can use code agents in many ways. An oversimplification I use is the
AI code-generation spectrum. A line where, on the left you have *vibe-coding*,
the "technique" where you let AI make all design and implementation decisions
in the system. On the right, you have *human-coding*, where zero code was generated
by AI.

*AI-aided software engineering* lies somewhere between this line. For the purposes
of this post, that is not important. My complaints earlier will apply mostly to
vibe-coded software.
